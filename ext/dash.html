Seeing a break coming

Before an ad starts, Twitch quietly takes the quality you are watching out of the stream's quality list, forcing the player down. That happens four to twelve seconds before the first ad exists, and it is the only warning anything gives us. When we see it we stop following the player and hold the clean feed instead.

Where the video came from

Unslop does not filter ads out of your stream. It runs its own clean sessions in the background and replaces the playlist wholesale, because a session that has taken an ad is permanently out of step with one that hasn't.

Why we ever miss

Two different failures, and telling them apart is the whole game. A shortage means the background sessions had nothing clean. A reset means we threw our own buffer away — usually chasing the player as it changes quality — and were caught cold. The second one is our bug, not Twitch's doing.

Sessions retired — A background session took an ad. Once that happens its video no longer lines up with the rest, so it's dropped and replaced.
Quality changes followed — Each one currently resets the buffer, which takes ~20s to refill.
Quality flapping — Changes that reversed within seconds. High numbers here mean the resets above were wasted.

How this works

Twitch stitches ads into the same video stream as the content, from the same servers, under the same names. There is no request to block.

The swap
Unslop opens a few extra sessions to the same channel in the background. When Twitch drops an ad into yours, at least one of those is usually still carrying clean video, and we answer your player with that instead.
Why not just delete the ads
Ads replace content rather than delaying it, and Twitch re-cuts the stream where the ad break ended. A session that takes an ad comes back about 1.4 seconds out of step and never recovers, so its own video can't be stitched back onto the part you missed.
How we see a break coming
Seconds before an ad starts, the quality you are watching disappears from the stream's quality list and the player is pushed down to 360p. Nothing else announces a break. When it happens we stop following the player and keep serving the quality we already have buffered, which is the difference between covering a break and walking into it.
Why it needs a moment after you join
The background sessions have to build up a few seconds of video before there is anything to swap in. Until then you get Twitch's stream, ads included — a visible ad beats a black screen.
Why quality changes cost you
The buffer is per-quality. Changing quality throws it away and starts again, and an ad break is exactly when the player is most likely to change quality.
Two streams at once
Each open stream gets its own background sessions and its own counters, out of one shared budget. Leave a stream paused or closed and it stops asking for playlists; half a minute later its sessions are released to whoever is still watching.
